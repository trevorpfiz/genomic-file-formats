Genomics runs on text files and their compressed, indexed relatives. This course teaches you to recognize each common format and to judge whether a file is valid. A broken file should stop you before it misleads an analysis.

The course objective, in the words of its author:

> Demonstrate understanding of different data formats and types by recognizing each file type and assessing its validity. Data formats to include FASTA, FASTQ, BED, BEDPE, WIG, GTF/GFF3, SAM, BAM, CRAM, gVCF, VCF, and GFA/PAF.

## One case, start to finish

The course follows one fictional family. Noa is four years old and has seizures that no one has explained yet. The files from her family's genetic testing carry the story. Each format answers one question on the way from a blood sample to a diagnosis.

::pipeline-map

The family is invented. The reference genome, the gene annotation and the known variant sites are real public data. The variants and genotypes in the story are made up.

## How this course works

- **Lessons.** Each lesson takes 8 to 10 minutes. Short stretches of reading alternate with exercises that check your understanding as you go.
- **Exercises.** Retry any exercise as often as you like. Hints and "Show answer" appear after a few attempts.
- **Read more.** Collapsed cards hold spec details and optional practice. No quiz question depends on them.
- **Section quizzes.** Each quiz has six questions, and you pass with five. Retakes draw new questions, and your best score is kept.
- **Capstone and badge.** At the end you triage a folder of case files. Pass every quiz and finish the capstone to earn the completion badge.

Your progress stays in this browser. The course sends nothing anywhere.
